# Common challenges — quick fixes that actually work

Working with Claude is like working with a helpful colleague: most of the time it’s smooth, and sometimes you need to nudge it. Below are the five things people run into first — each with a short, fix you can try immediately.

1) Response feels generic
- What's happening: Claude doesn't have enough specifics about your situation.
- Try this: Add context. Example: instead of "Write an email about the project delay," say "Write an apologetic email to our enterprise client about a two‑week integration delay; keep it professional and offer next steps."

2) Output is too long or too short
- What's happening: Claude is guessing length.
- Try this: Be explicit. "Give me a two‑paragraph summary" or "Keep it under 100 words." Want a list? Ask: "Give me 5 bullet points."

3) Format is wrong
- What's happening: Claude knows the idea but not the layout.
- Try this: Show an example. Paste a one‑line sample or say "Use bullets with bold headings for each section." Clear structure = predictable output.

4) Confident but wrong info
- What's happening: Claude can sound sure even when a fact is incorrect.
- Try this: Double‑check important facts. Ask Claude to cite sources or say "Mark uncertain points" so you can verify before acting.

5) Tone doesn't match
- What's happening: Default tone is helpful/professional but may not fit your audience.
- Try this: Tell Claude the voice you want: "Make this conversational and friendly" or "Make this formal and concise." Paste a one‑sentence example to lock the style.

Quick workflow to fix most issues
- Start with: role + task + format + tone (one short line). Example: "I'm a product manager. Summarize this doc in 5 bullets for execs — concise, professional."
- If the first draft isn't right: tweak one constraint (length, tone, or format) and ask for a second pass.
- When accuracy matters: attach sources and ask Claude to list assumptions.

Final tip (keeps things fun)
- Treat Claude like a teammate — brief direction, quick feedback, and a little patience. Small edits and follow-ups turn a rough draft into something useful fast.

Happy prompting — small prompts, big results.

## The iteration mindset — start small, improve fast

Think of your first prompt as the opening line of a conversation, not the final product. Treat Claude's first draft like a rough sketch: useful, but unfinished.

- Review quickly: pick one thing to change (length, tone, or format).
- Give targeted feedback: instead of "Make it shorter," try "Cut the first paragraph and make the ending action-oriented." 
- Know when to restart: if the thread drifts, a fresh chat with a clearer prompt is often faster.

Small, focused revisions compound into great results.

## AI Fluency — the 4D framework (short and practical)

AI Fluency is the skill of working well with AI. Here are four compact habits that make you fluent:

- **Delegate:** Decide what Claude should do vs. what you should keep. Use Claude for drafting, you for final judgment.
- **Describe:** Give clear instructions — role, audience, format, and examples.
- **Discern:** Check outputs critically. Ask: "Is this accurate? Is the tone right?"
- **Diligence:** Use AI responsibly — verify facts, keep transparency, and own final decisions.

These four habits help you get repeatable, reliable outcomes from Claude.

## Evals — test Claude where it matters

Want to know if Claude is actually good at a task? Run a quick eval:

1. Collect 5–10 real examples of the work you do (emails, reports, analyses).
2. Write test prompts that mimic your real requests.
3. Run Claude and compare outputs to your examples. Ask:
	- Does it capture key info?
	- Is tone and structure correct?
	- What’s missing?
4. Improve prompts or add examples until results are reliable.

Evals are simple, fast, and tell you where Claude saves time — and where human review is essential.
